# 6. VÈO GIAO — PRODUCT THESIS

Core idea:
Vèo Giao acts as a digitalization layer over local delivery operations.

Product principles:
- Manual before unnecessary automation.
- Phone before unnecessary in-app chat.
- Simple and understandable economics.
- Drivers must never personally finance COD.
- Money movement must be understandable: who caused it, why, and how much changed.
- Cost-generating features need conceptual limits/safety boundaries.
- Reuse established product mechanisms.
- Product truth is separate from implementation.

Portfolio narrative:
The interesting design challenge is not “build another food-delivery app.” It is adapting marketplace, logistics, financial, and operational mechanisms to rural conditions where assumptions from large urban platforms may fail.

# 8. VÈO GIAO — SIGNATURE PRODUCT SYSTEMS

## 8.1 Fair Driver Dispatch

Key mechanism:
7-second anti-sniping window.

Product rationale:
Drivers need enough time to understand an offer rather than losing it instantly to whoever taps first.

Information considered important:
- Address
- COD
- Earnings / delivery value

Portfolio theme:
Marketplace fairness and driver UX, not simply “order notification.”

---

## 8.2 Driver Financial Model

The financial model evolved through product decisions.

Important final/evolved direction captured in project material:
- Driver pays merchant cash at pickup.
- Driver collects customer cash.
- Merchant does not need a platform wallet in the simplified model.
- Driver wallet is primarily a prepaid operating/regional fee account.
- Driver wallet should not go zero/negative.
- Exhausted balance blocks new orders.
- Active deliveries can finish before the driver goes offline.

Earlier concept:
Driver collateral/float and closed-loop balances were considered to ensure the driver did not personally finance COD.

Portfolio theme:
Designing financial safety around physical cash logistics and driver liquidity.

Important:
Present this as an evolving product decision, not as one unchanging design.

---

## 8.3 Financial Transparency

Product requirements:
- Balance changes should have understandable reasons.
- Financial rules can be snapshotted at order creation.
- Merchant, driver, regional, and platform economics should be distinguishable.
- Voucher subsidies should be traceable.
- Regional budgets should be protected.

Portfolio theme:
Money movement was treated as a product experience, not only as backend accounting.

---

## 8.4 Regional Prepaid Safety Lock

When an operating balance is exhausted:
- New customer ordering can be blocked.
- Merchant writes can be blocked.
- Read/reconciliation access remains available.
- Recovery occurs through funding.

Portfolio theme:
A regional operating safety mechanism that prevents uncontrolled cost exposure while keeping visibility/reconciliation possible.

---

## 8.5 Merchant Capability-Based Access

Roles/capabilities include:
- Cashier
- Kitchen
- Manager
- Accountant
- Custom capabilities
- OWNER across stores

Important behavior:
- Permissions can be limited by store.
- Sensitive customer data can be masked.
- Revealing sensitive information should be intentional.
- Sensitive actions should be auditable.
- UI behavior should reflect actual capabilities.

Portfolio theme:
Permission design based on business capabilities rather than simple role labels.

# 24. PORTFOLIO-WORTHY PRODUCT DECISIONS

Use these as “decision cards” or visual callouts:

### Decision A — 7 Seconds Before Acceptance
Problem:
Instant order sniping creates unfair driver competition.

Decision:
Give drivers a short reading/decision window.

Why it matters:
Fairness + better informed acceptance.

### Decision B — Protect Driver Liquidity
Problem:
Drivers should not personally finance COD.

Decision:
Separate delivery cash movement from the driver's operating balance.

Why it matters:
Protects drivers from uncontrolled cash exposure.

### Decision C — Block New Work, Preserve Visibility
Problem:
Regional operating funds can be exhausted.

Decision:
Stop new cost-generating work while keeping read/reconciliation access.

Why it matters:
Safety without destroying operational visibility.

### Decision D — Capability-Based Merchant Access
Problem:
Store teams have different responsibilities.

Decision:
Use business capabilities and store scope rather than broad role assumptions.

Why it matters:
Better security and clearer operations.

### Decision E — Remove the 300m Hard Gate
Problem:
Strict proximity enforcement interfered with batched deliveries and exact location updates.

Decision:
Do not unnecessarily block operational completion based on a rigid proximity gate.

Why it matters:
Real-world delivery flow beats theoretical perfection.

### Decision F — Cache Historical Data
Problem:
Repeated history requests create unnecessary API traffic.

Decision:
Use server-side filtering/pagination plus caching for historical data, while preserving realtime behavior for active orders.

Why it matters:
Better performance and lower operational cost, especially under weak connectivity.
